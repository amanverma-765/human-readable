## 1. Executive Summary & Document Overview

This document provides an exhaustive, line-by-line extraction and synthesis of the research paper *"Why is AI-generated language so hard to understand?"*. 

The paper addresses a ubiquitous yet previously unquantified phenomenon: the widespread perception that text generated by modern frontier large language models (LLMs)—often informally termed **"Claudish"** or "AI dialect"—has become increasingly unnatural, exhausting, and difficult for humans to comprehend. 

While existing literature has examined surface-level AI mannerisms (such as the overuse of specific buzzwords like *"delve"* or dense Latinate vocabulary), this work presents the first rigorous psycholinguistic investigation into the **syntactic and structural properties** driving human processing difficulty in AI text.

### Key Takeaways at a Glance
1. **Empirical Confirmation of Processing Difficulty (Experiment 1):** Frontier models (including Claude Sonnet 5, Opus 5, Opus 5.5, Fable 5; GPT-5.6 Sol, Terra, Luna; GLM-5; Kimi K2.5) systematically overuse linguistic constructions known in cognitive science to overload human working memory and trigger parsing reanalysis:
   - Long syntactic dependencies
   - Stacked noun compounds (3+ nouns modifying each other)
   - Inanimate subjects with active verbs that typically require animate subjects (selectional restriction violations)
   - Ad-hoc denominal verbs (nouns converted into verbs)
   - Reduced object relative clauses (systematically dropping optional function words like *"that"*)
   - Unattested $n$-gram sequences and high trigram perplexity / surprisal.
2. **Semantic Decoupling via Bilingual Pivoting:** By generating parallel paraphrases through round-trip machine translation (English $\to$ Japanese $\to$ English using NLLB-200), the authors prove that these difficult features are **not driven by semantic necessity or topical complexity**, but represent an explicit stylistic and syntactic choice made by post-trained LLMs over easier, natural alternatives.
3. **Causal Mechanism Identified (Experiment 2):** Why does this occur? Human natural language evolved under dual constraints: ease of production and ease of comprehension (Uniform Information Density). In contrast, frontier LLMs undergo post-training via **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)** for code, math, and multi-step reasoning, where rewards reward correctness regardless of communicative clarity, while operating under token costs and finite context limits.
4. **Reinforcement Learning Length-Penalty Proof-of-Concept:** By training Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) under varying length penalties ($\lambda$) and KL-divergence constraints ($\beta$), the authors causally demonstrate that a length penalty creates an emergent, near-categorical preference for compressed syntactic alternations (omitting optional words, switching to double-object datives). Crucially, this compression preference **bleeds into untrained constructions (near and far transfer)** and degrades out-of-domain open-ended text.
5. **Evolutionary Trajectory & Remedies:** Base pre-frontier models do not exhibit these pathological compression traits. The traits emerge and amplify across post-training stages (Base $\to$ Instruct $\to$ Reasoning/Think). However, Anthropic's release of Claude Opus 5.5 demonstrates that intentional post-training alignment can reverse this degradation, showing lower processing difficulty on 19 of 21 comparisons compared to Opus 5.

## 2. The Core Problem Statement & Research Questions

### The Phenomenon of "Claudish" and AI Intelligibility
Despite models achieving state-of-the-art benchmark performances on standardized reasoning, coding, and question-answering benchmarks, human users consistently report that reading long AI responses feels cognitive-load heavy, stiff, and convoluted (anecdotes cited notably from University of Waterloo, 2026).

### The Evolutionary Puzzle
Natural language is shaped by human communication. Why would models trained on billions of tokens of human writing develop structural features that actively impair human comprehension?

### Motivating Example Analyzed in the Paper
The authors craft a representative sentence embodying modern LLM writing:
> *"Your instinct is right; the convoluted sentence you are currently reading is quietly eating its own reader-patience budget — footnote it."*

Deconstruction of its psycholinguistic failure modes:
1. **Reduced Object Relative Clause:** *"the convoluted sentence [that] you are currently reading"* (optional relativizer dropped).
2. **Selectional Restriction Mismatch:** *"sentence ... is quietly eating"* (an inanimate abstract noun acting as the subject of an animate physical consumption verb).
3. **Stacked Noun Compound:** *"reader-patience budget"* (three nouns stacked in modifier relations, introducing bracket ambiguity).
4. **Ad-hoc Denominal Verb:** *"footnote it"* (converting the noun *"footnote"* into an imperative verb).
5. **Extended Dependency Length:** Non-local syntactic dependencies spanning across modifying clauses.

### Core Hypotheses
* **Hypothesis 1 (H1 - Manifestation):** The language output of frontier and near-frontier LLMs disproportionately overuses linguistic constructions associated with human comprehension difficulty relative to human reference text and meaning-preserving paraphrases.
* **Hypothesis 2 (H2 - Mechanism):** A pressure for information density and token compression during post-training (math, code, reasoning, context limits) shifts LLM language generation away from human communicative optimization toward compressed, difficult structures.

### The Architectural Asymmetry: Humans vs. Modern LLMs
* **Human Processing Constraints:** Lossy, distance-sensitive short-term memory (Lewis 1996; Futrell et al. 2020), strict bounded cognitive capacity, and real-time incremental processing. Human language evolved communicative efficiency (Gibson et al. 2019) to balance production ease with comprehension success.
* **LLM Architecture & Training Dynamics:** Full bidirectional self-attention across massive context windows (100k+ to 1M+ tokens; Vaswani et al. 2017). LLMs experience **zero memory decay over distance** (Oh & Linzen 2026; Kuribayashi et al. 2022). Consequently, long dependencies and complex non-local structures carry zero penalty for the model, while costing human readers dearly.

## 5. Feature Detection Mechanics & The Paraphrase Control Paradigm

### 5.1 The Seven Psycholinguistic Measures

1. **Object Relative Clauses (ORC) - Without Relativizer:** Count of relative clauses modifying an object noun where the relative pronoun (*"that"*, *"which"*, *"who"*) is omitted (e.g., *"the car [that] he drove"*).
2. **Object Relative Clauses (ORC) - With Relativizer:** Count of ORCs where the overt relativizer is explicitly retained.
3. **Denominal Verbs:** Nouns converted into verbs (e.g., *"calendar the meeting"*, *"dotfolder"*). Identified via spaCy `en_core_web_trf` candidates (verbs whose lemma is predominantly a noun in human reference corpora) and confirmed by the external classifier **Jev 1.13.0** with threshold $P > 0.5$.
4. **Animate Verbs with Inanimate Subjects:** Violation of selectional restrictions where active verbs typically requiring agency take inanimate/abstract subjects (e.g., *"the check covered str but forgot bytes"*, *"asymmetry bites"*, *"chaos expects"*). Extracted via dependency parse candidates and verified by Jev 1.13.0 ($P > 0.5$).
5. **Standardized Dependency Length:** Distance in words between syntactically connected words. Because raw dependency length scales mechanically with sentence length, the authors developed an epidemiological **direct standardization** technique:
   * Sentences binned into 5 word-length buckets: 1–5, 6–10, 11–15, 16–20, and 21–30 words (sentences >30 words dropped; root and punctuation excluded).
   * Bins reweighted according to the sentence-length distribution of Wikipedia.
   * Standardized value: *"If every model had the exact sentence-length distribution of Wikipedia, what would its average dependency length be?"*
6. **Stacked Noun Compounds:** Sequences of 3 or more contiguous nouns acting in a modifier relationship (e.g., *"brokerage money market funds"*, *"class-time shutdown requirement"*).
7. **$N$-Gram Rarity Statistics:**
   * **Unattested Word Pairs:** Number of bigrams per 1,000 pairs never appearing in the reference human corpus.
   * **Trigram Surprisal / Perplexity:** Scored using KenLM with modified Kneser–Ney smoothing against domain-matched corpora: OpenSubtitles (chat), StackExchange (coding), and Wikipedia (essays).

### 5.2 The Semantic Control: Bilingual Round-Trip Translation
A fundamental methodological hurdle in analyzing style is semantic confounding: perhaps AI models use difficult language simply because they discuss more technical, complex topics than average humans.

To isolate **syntax from semantics**, the authors employed **bilingual pivoting (round-trip translation)**:
$$\text{English Source Sentence} \xrightarrow{\text{NLLB-200 (3.3B)}} \text{Japanese} \xrightarrow{\text{NLLB-200 (3.3B)}} \text{English Paraphrase}$$
* **Why Japanese?** Japanese is typologically distant from English (SOV word order, agglutinative, head-final), forcing the translation model to decompose and re-synthesize English syntactic structures from scratch rather than performing literal word-for-word substitution.
* **Why NLLB-200?** Machine translation models naturally regress to high-frequency, standard syntactic conventions, stripping idiosyncrasies while preserving semantic propositions.
* **Validation & Robustness:** Repeated across a Russian pivot and using TranslateGemma 4B (Appendix B.5, Figure 10), confirming identical trends.
* **Inference Logic:** If a linguistic feature drops sharply after round-trip translation, the feature was **not semantically necessary**; the LLM actively chose the complex construction over a simpler paraphrase.

### 6.2 The Relativizer Asymmetry (The "Token Compression" Fingerprint)
One of the most striking findings is the diametric divergence between relative clauses with and without relativizers:
* Across almost all frontier models, **reduced ORCs (without "that") drop significantly under round-trip translation**, meaning models use them far more than natural paraphrases.
* Conversely, **overt ORCs (with "that") increase under round-trip translation** (Figure 4, Figure 6).
* **Insight:** LLMs exhibit a systematic bias against optional grammatical markers. They strip *"that"* to minimize token length, directly violating human **Uniform Information Density (UID)** strategies where *"that"* is inserted to protect the listener from comprehension bottlenecks.

### 6.3 Domain Dynamics: Multi-Turn Accumulation
* The prevalence of processing-heavy features was significantly higher in **multi-turn Chat and Coding** than in single-turn **Essays** (Figures 3a and 6).
* **Explanation:** Multi-turn interactions generate growing context windows. As context expands, computational and attention pressures compound, incentivizing models to produce tighter, information-compressed sentences to fit within conversational budgets.

### 6.4 The Post-Training Evolutionary Hierarchy
By comparing models at different stages of training, the paper charts the emergence of the AI dialect:
1. **Pre-Frontier Base Models (Llama 3.1 8B Base, Olmo 3 7B Base):**
   * Exhibit syntactic profiles closest to human text.
   * Show virtually no change under round-trip translation (Figure 2, Figure 4).
   * **Rule:** Base pre-training on human text does *not* create "Claudish".
2. **Supervised Fine-Tuning / Instruct Models (Llama Instruct, Olmo Instruct):**
   * Moderate increase in compression features and rare $n$-grams.
3. **Reasoning / RL Models (Olmo 3 7B Think):**
   * Shows a massive, dramatic leap in difficult features, clustering directly with the $100\times$ larger frontier models!
   * Proves that **reasoning-focused post-training (RL)** is the primary driver of linguistic unnaturalness.
4. **Frontier Flagships (GPT-5.6 Sol, Claude Opus 5, Claude Fable 5):**
   * Display the highest density of denominal verbs, stacked nouns, selectional restriction violations, and long dependencies.

### 6.5 The Case of Claude Opus 5.5: Proof of Reversibility
A critical empirical finding is the comparison between Anthropic’s **Claude Opus 5** and **Claude Opus 5.5**:
* Claude Opus 5.5 showed lower rates of difficulty-linked features on **19 out of 21 comparisons** across chat, coding, and essays.
* In **8 of those comparisons**, the 95% bootstrap confidence intervals did not even overlap with Opus 5.
* **Significance:** This demonstrates that the AI dialect is not an inevitable consequence of scale, but a malleable outcome of post-training reward design. Intentional alignment can successfully restore human-like communicative features.

## 8. Experiment 2: Causal Mechanism via Reinforcement Learning Under Length Penalties

To move from correlation to causation (testing **Hypothesis 2**), the authors designed a controlled reinforcement learning experiment to isolate how length penalties alter grammatical preferences.

### 8.1 Experimental Task Formulation
* **Base Architecture:** `Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct`
* **Adaptation Method:** LoRA (PEFT 0.21.0), rank $r=16$, $\alpha=32$ across all linear layers.
* **Task:** Convert JSON-formatted structured event descriptions into fluent, past-tense English sentences.
  * *Input Example:* `{"theme": "a photo", "agent": "he", "event": "send", "recipient": "us"}`
  * *Option A (Short, 6 tokens):* *"He sent us a photo."* (Double Object - DO)
  * *Option B (Long, 7 tokens):* *"He sent a photo to us."* (Prepositional Object - PO)

### 8.2 Mathematical Framework & GRPO Formulation
The policy is trained via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO; Shao et al. 2024) using TRL 1.13.0 to maximize the KL-regularized expected reward:
$$J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{D}, y \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|x)} [R(x, y)] - \beta \, \mathbb{E}_x \left[ \text{KL}\left(\pi_\theta(\cdot|x) \,\parallel\, \pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot|x)\right) \right]$$

#### The Reward Function:
$$R(x, y) = c(x, y) \cdot f(y) - \lambda |y|$$
Where:
* $c(x, y) \in \{0, 1\}$ is rule-based correctness: every argument phrase must appear verbatim in the correct dependency role (checked via spaCy `en_core_web_trf`), and the verb lemma must be preserved. Both syntactic variants are completely valid and receive $c(x, y) = 1$.
* $f(y) \in \{0, 1\}$ is formatting compliance: single sentence, capital letter, ends with punctuation, no preamble.
* $|y|$ is the completion length in tokens.
* $\lambda$ is the per-token length penalty.

#### Theoretical Optimal Policy Distribution:
For two variants (short $s$ and long $l$) differing by $\Delta = |l| - |s|$ tokens that achieve identical task reward, the closed-form optimum policy satisfies:
$$\log \frac{\pi^*(s)}{\pi^*(l)} = \log \frac{\pi_{\text{ref}}(s)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(l)} + \frac{\lambda \Delta}{\beta}$$
* When $\beta > 0$, the log-odds shift smoothly in proportion to $\frac{\lambda \Delta}{\beta}$.
* When $\beta = 0$ (no KL regularization), no stable interior optimum exists; the policy collapses completely to the shortest sequence allowed by training duration.

#### Advantage & Loss Without Length Bias:
* Advantage computed with group-mean baseline without standard-deviation division ($A_i = R_i - \frac{1}{G}\sum R_j$), preventing artificial inflation of minuscule length differences when all completions in a group are correct.
* Loss implemented via `dr_grpo` (Liu et al. 2025): per-token clipped surrogates summed and divided by a batch constant ($N \cdot L_{\max}$), preventing per-sequence length normalization artifacts.

* **KL Divergence Coefficient:** $\beta \in \{0.0, 0.04\}$

* **Compute:** 500 training steps (4,000 prompts total), 128 completions per step ($G=16$, micro-batch 32). NVIDIA L40S (48GB), ~31 GPU hours total.

### 8.4 Empirical Findings of Experiment 2

#### 1. Causal Emergence of Syntactic Compression
As $\lambda$ increases, the probability of selecting the shorter syntactic variant rises monotonically across all trained alternations (Figure 7B). The model was **never instructed to be concise or prefer specific grammar**; compression emerged strictly from reinforcement optimization.

#### 2. The Catastrophic Effect of Removing KL Regularization ($\beta = 0$)
* At $\beta = 0$, for any $\lambda > 0$, the model underwent **categorical collapse**: the short variant was chosen in **over 98% of all completions**.
* This completely destroys human-like linguistic variation. Humans choose between variants dynamically based on cognitive accessibility, rhythm, and information predictability. The model, lacking KL constraint, flattened language into a mechanical, hyper-compressed monoculture.
* When $\beta = 0.04$, the shift was smooth and graded, perfectly matching theoretical predictions.

#### 3. The Particle Movement Control Proves Length is the Sole Driver
In the particle alternation (*"picked up the book"* vs. *"picked the book up"*), both options have identical token lengths ($\Delta = 0$). Across all values of $\lambda$ and $\beta$, **the preference between particle orders remained completely unchanged**. This provides definitive proof that the shift was driven purely by token conservation, not by arbitrary grammatical bias during training.

#### 4. Near and Far Transfer (Generalization Beyond Training)
* **Near Transfer:** Models trained under length penalties immediately generalized to dropping optional *"that"* in **adjective complements** and **purpose clauses** (Figure 7C), despite never seeing those constructions during fine-tuning.
* **Far Transfer:** When $\beta = 0$, the compression bias generalized to the **Saxon genitive** (*"X's Y"* over *"Y of X"*). Other far-transfer structures (passive vs. active) did not shift, and quantifiers (*"all the"*) were already saturated in the base model.

#### 5. Bleed-Through into Open-Ended Generation (Figure 9)
When tested on 40 completely open-ended prompts unrelated to the JSON training task:
* Models trained with higher $\lambda$ generated responses with **higher trigram surprisal** (bits).
* **Unattested trigram rates rose significantly**, matching the frontier model profiles in Experiment 1.
* Words per response declined systematically.
* **Insight:** Task-specific optimization under token constraints fundamentally alters the global language model distribution, bleeding into open-domain writing.

## 9. Extracted Rules, Principles, and Mechanistic Insights

From the combined findings of Experiments 1 and 2, we extract the core governing laws of AI language evolution:

### Rule 1: The Verifiable Reward Trade-off (RLVR Dilemma)
In RLHF, human evaluators historically rewarded length and detail, creating "verbosity bias". In the industry-wide shift toward RLVR (DeepSeek-R1, agentic coding benchmarks, math reasoning), rewards are purely functional (tests pass, math matches ground truth). Humans are removed from the loop. In the absence of human communicative feedback, models evolve an insular, cryptic dialect optimized for problem-solving efficiency rather than human legibility.

### Rule 2: Token Economy Breeds Cognitive Debt
Every token saved in model output reduces inference latency and API expenditure for AI labs, but shifts a heavy cognitive burden onto the human reader. Stripping optional function words (*"that"*), substituting compound nouns for prepositional phrases, and using dense denominal verbs force human readers to perform costly syntactic reanalysis.

### Rule 3: The Superhuman Memory Mismatch
Because Transformer architectures maintain lossless attention across 100k+ tokens, long-distance dependencies and center-embedded clauses carry zero processing difficulty for the LLM. The model has no intrinsic pressure to minimize dependency length. Without explicit constraints, LLMs will generate syntactic structures that easily overload bounded human working memory.

### Rule 4: Selectional Restrictions Erode Under Metaphorical Compression
Models compress multi-word explanatory phrases into compact subject-verb units by violating animacy restrictions (e.g. converting *"this condition causes an error"* into *"the check bites"* or *"is_file() swallows that"*). While semantically parsable, these violations trigger P600 ERP reanalysis spikes in human brains.

### Rule 5: KL Regularization Is the Bulwark Against Dialectal Collapse
Without an explicit KL penalty anchor ($\beta > 0$) tied to natural human pre-training distributions, models subjected to length penalties or verifiable rewards undergo categorical collapse, eradicating natural syntactic diversity.

## 10. Practical Engineering Implications & Guidelines

For AI researchers, model trainers, and prompt engineers developing frontier systems:

### 1. Architectural & Post-Training Interventions
* **Incorporate Psycholinguistic Reward Terms:** Post-training reward functions should explicitly penalize known processing bottlenecks:
  $$R_{\text{total}} = R_{\text{task}} - \lambda_{\text{len}}|y| - \gamma_1 \cdot \text{DepLen}(y) - \gamma_2 \cdot \text{StackedNouns}(y) - \gamma_3 \cdot \text{SurprisalVariance}(y)$$
* **Uniform Information Density Preference Optimization (UID-PO):** As proposed by Li & Xu (2026), train preference models that reward smooth, uniform information transfer rather than extreme local compression.
* **Maintain Bounded KL Regularization:** Always enforce $\beta > 0$ during RLVR and reasoning fine-tuning to prevent the categorical loss of optional function words.

### 2. Prompt Engineering & System Design Remedies
When deploying models in production for human-facing documentation, communication, or customer support:
* **Anti-Claudish Prompt Directives:** Explicitly instruct models to avoid dense syntactic compression:
  ```text
  Guidelines for clarity and human readability:
  - Use explicit relative pronouns (prefer "the proposal that we discussed" over "the proposal we discussed").
  - Avoid stacking three or more nouns; unpack noun compounds using prepositional phrases (e.g. use "budget for patient care" instead of "patient care budget").
  - Do not use inanimate objects as subjects for human action verbs (avoid "the script swallows the exception"; write "the script handles the exception").
  - Prefer established verbs over ad-hoc denominal conversions (write "add it to the calendar", not "calendar it").
  - Keep sentences syntactically direct and minimize long-distance dependent clauses.
  ```

### 3. Monitoring & Quality Assurance
* **Psycholinguistic Linters:** Integrate dependency length and compound noun analyzers (using spaCy or similar parsers) into CI/CD evaluation pipelines alongside BLEU, ROUGE, and LLM-as-a-judge benchmarks to monitor the human intelligibility of model releases.

## 12. Conclusion

The paper *"Why is AI-generated language so hard to understand?"* provides a groundbreaking link between cognitive linguistics and modern reinforcement learning. The authors prove that the dense, unnatural, and exhausting feel of frontier AI text is not an illusion, but the measurable consequence of optimizing models for non-communicative reasoning and token efficiency. 

As language models transition from chatbots to autonomous reasoning agents, their linguistic environment diverges from the human evolutionary crucible. Unless research teams consciously incorporate psycholinguistic ease-of-processing objectives into post-training reward architectures, future AI language will continue to drift into an alien dialect—efficient for machines, but impenetrable to human minds.
